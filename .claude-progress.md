**What was done**

- **Zero mock data.** The store used to seed a 21-dish fixture café into every

  by nothing that runs.
- **Dev-login** at `POST /api/auth/dev-login` — `dev@costbook.test` /
  `costbook-dev`. 404s in production, verified against a real production build.

**Decisions made**

- No email confirmation for now. A31's verify screen stays built but out of the
  path until a real mail provider is configured; the free tier's sender would
  put an inbox round-trip in front of every test.
- An account starts empty and every app screen sends an unfinished one to the
  wizard. Nobody meets a dashboard reading "0 dishes · average food cost —".
- The org is created with an empty name rather than a guessed one. Deriving a
  café name from an email address is the same class of invention as guessing a
  rate.

**Bugs found and fixed**

- `.field` collision — sign-in fields drew two borders. `app.css`'s
  `.field input` (0,1,1) beat `entry.css`'s `.field-input` (0,1,0), so
  `border: 0` lost on specificity regardless of source order.
- A quantity of zero passed component construction, was stored, and only threw
  at costing time — inside a `useMemo`, so the whole page fell to the error
  boundary. Both builders validate it now, and `tryRecipeCost` keeps a bad row
  from blanking the dashboard, the library or the cost sheet.
- `changed_by` had no `ON DELETE` clause, so a user who had ever changed a rate
  could never be deleted — contradicting the privacy page's own promise.
- The landing page claimed its illustration was "a real café's menu". It was
  not. Relabelled as examples.
- `admin@thebrewapps.com` came from a global rules file belonging to another
  project and was creating real accounts in Costbook's database. Removed from
  the whole environment (28 files), with a rule added so it cannot recur.

## 2026-09-03 — Authentication: flow, sign in, sign up, plan buying

**What was done**: The four auth items in the order the user set. Full-project
audit first (601 tests passing, typecheck and build clean), then four commits.

- **Flow.** `Book` gains `userId` and `role` — the caller's own, found by id
  through the new `roleOf`. `afterSignIn` had been reading `members[0].role`
  and calling it theirs; `members` is every membership row in whatever order
  Postgres returns, with pending invitations concatenated, so a manager was
  routed as the owner about as often as not. Two more copies of the same guess
  in the setup page and the flag action. `requireRole` added and applied to the
  seven owner-only Settings actions, which is the check `proxy.ts` and the RLS
  migration both promised in comments while `canDo` sat with zero callers.
  Migration 11 drops `membership_bootstrap`.
- **Sign in.** `triesLeft` is `number | null`; the Supabase path returned 0 and
  the form printed "0 tries left before we lock the account for 15 minutes" on
  the first wrong password. The `/sign-in/link` button pointed at a route that
  never existed. `/reset` deleted — it was three local steps ending in
  `router.push('/dashboard')`, so a forgotten password had no recovery at all.
- **Sign up.** "Email me a sign-in link" called `setSent(email)` and nothing
  else, then claimed an account existed and mail had been sent. Removed;
  `resend` is a real `supabase.auth.resend`.
- **Plan buying.** Free tier 40 → 10 and actually enforced on both create paths
  (`atFreeLimit` had no callers). `choosePlan` removed — an exported server
  action is a public endpoint, and it was a free upgrade. `savePlan` writes the
  subscriptions row. `chooseCurrency` and the setup page were reading the
  in-memory fixture instead of the account.

**Decisions made**: No domain yet, so no mail — magic link and password reset
are removed rather than faked, and return next phase. Free tier is 10 because
it is for evaluation. Price centralised as `PAID_MONTHLY` (₹750), read by the
landing and Settings. Where a figure was not known it is a dash, not a number.

  ingredient list back to its state 30 days ago — every `RateChange` carries

  previewed changes nobody had made, about ones that already happened. 20 tests.
- **Recipes** gained `worstFirst` as a second ordering beside the category

**Decisions made**: Rolling 30-day window rather than a per-user seen marker

every dish above it uncostable, so `impactOf` reported those dishes as having

not brought to Recipes: a fifth column through four grid variants for a
comparison the sorted percentage already makes.

organising principle of Recipes, and the category filter has one option.
Import is now paid-only and the dev account is free, so re-importing needs the
subscriptions row set to paid first.

## 2026-09-03 — Entry flows: recipes and ingredients, for a hand on a keyboard

**Brief**: think like a designer for a chef or data-entry person. Easy to
start, easy to end, not over-clicked. Look "very carefully" at add-dish and
add-ingredient.

**What the audit found** (walked in the running app, not read):
- The Ingredients entry row was already the right pattern — Tab across, Enter
  commits, row clears and refocuses, duplicates caught before they exist.
- Recipe lines had **no typed quantity anywhere**. A picked ingredient started
  at 1 kg and the only control was a −/+ stepper on the stored gram figure in
  steps of 50/10/5/1 — ~30 presses to reach 250 g. Deliberate for a wet finger
  on a tablet (A13); wrong for a hand on a keyboard entering a recipe.
- The Add-line drawer printed ₹ on an AED account: `lib/picker.ts` formatted
  its rate with `ORG.currencySymbol` — the demo fixture from `lib/data.ts`.
- Every `Sheet` stole focus on parent re-render: `[open, onClose]` as effect
  deps, inline `onClose` recreated each render, `panel.focus()` inside.

**What was built**:
- The drawer asks "How much?" at pick time. Type "250 g", Enter — the line
  lands, the drawer stays open, focus returns to search. Enter on an empty
  field is the old one-press 1 kg. `addComponent` takes an amount; a unit
  from the wrong family ("250 ml" of a thing bought by weight) is refused in
  words, never costed on a guess. 4 tests.
- A typed quantity beside the stepper on every expanded line. Beside, never
  instead: the stepper stays for tablets.
- "500 g" / "2 l" typed into the ingredient row's Pack qty sets quantity and
  unit (grams and millilitres convert to a fraction of the kg/l pack).
- Picker rows carry a figure; the screen formats it in the currency in force.
- `Sheet` holds `onClose` in a ref and focuses once, on opening.

**Decisions**: never remove the stepper; add typing beside it. Parse typed
quantities with `core/loose.ts`, not a second parser. Gulf currency codes are
prefix with a space ("AED 83").

**Verified on the production build on :3000**: pick → focus in the field →
"250 g" Enter → 20 lines, last 250 → focus back in search → Escape closes.
Nothing saved.

**Next**: the new-dish screen's end of flow (land on the sheet with the paste
already priced — mostly there), and the same audit on Settings.

## 2026-09-03 — New dish, as a guided flow

**Brief**: when adding a new dish the user must understand what is going on
and what to do without anyone's help.

**What changed**: `/recipes/new` is four named steps with a rail that shows
where you are — Name it · What goes in it · Check · Create — each with one
sentence saying what it is for. "Show me an example" shows a pasted recipe
and what Costbook reads from it before anything is typed. Step 3 summarises
what was understood as chips (ready / linked to a batch / new / need you) and
every unclear line carries its own "How much?" or "Which unit?" field; a fix
is written back into the paste, so a fixed line and a right-first-time line
are the same thing by the time they are saved. The button says exactly what
will happen; the line under it says where you land. Enter in the name moves
to the paste; ⌘/Ctrl-Enter creates.

**Verified on the production build**: rail 1→2 on Enter, 2→3 when lines
land, held at 3 while a line still needs a unit; "100 g" typed on a flagged
row drops "2 need you" to "1 need you". Create not pressed; nothing written.

**Next**: the same walk on Settings.

## 2026-09-03 — The cost sheet, read by a cook

**Brief**: the user walked Koottu's sheet and could not read it. "0.03 l" —
is that ml? "Food cost ▲ 33.6%" — what does that mean? "0.77 ÷ 30.0% = 2.56,
AED 2.90 at 26.5%, Use / Keep" — "I just can't understand these
calculations." That is the bar for every figure on this screen.

**What changed**:
- **Quantities in cook's units.** `shownQty` in `lib/format.ts`: grams below
  a kilo, millilitres below a litre, the big unit at or above. Stored figures
  untouched. The table, the typed field and the stepper all speak the same
  unit, so "40" typed against "30 ml" means 40 ml. 6 tests.
- **Food cost, in a sentence** under the chip: "AED 34 of every AED 100 you
  charge for this goes on ingredients. You aimed for AED 30 — this plate is
  over."
- **Wastage and packaging say where they come from.** The chip reads FROM
  SETTINGS or THIS DISH, with a note naming the figures and both ways to
  change them (here for this dish, Settings for every dish). Settings already
  holds these on the Costing tab; nothing new was added there.
- **The pricing block is a story**: what you want to keep, what the plate
  costs, the exact price that hits it, why it is rounded and how, what each
  rounding leaves you keeping, what it sells at today — and the buttons say
  "Change the price to AED 2.90" / "Leave it at AED 2.29".
- **"Used in N recipes" steps back** on the expanded row; the batch/portion
  switch is the thing a cook is there to settle.

**Verified on the production build**: every line in g/ml; expanded Ghee reads
30 ml in field and stepper; "40" + Enter → 40 ml, unsaved. Rail sentences as
above.

**Next**: Settings, same walk. Then the same plain-language pass on the
Ingredients row and the import wizard.
